Receipts Express has no accounts and no servers. Your receipt images, the details read from them, and your expense reports are created and stored entirely on your own device, and are never sent to the author or to anyone else.

Nobody working on this project can see your data, because it never reaches the author. The rest of this page explains exactly what is stored, where, and what the few remaining edges are.

1.What this policy covers

This policy covers the Receipts Express web app, an installable Progressive Web App served from GitHub Pages. It is provided by Jordan Campbell ("the author"), an individual resident in the State of Washington, United States. It is an independent personal project, not a service operated by or on behalf of any employer or organization.

It does not cover what happens to a PDF, CSV, or backup file after you export it and save or send it somewhere else — at that point the file is yours to handle under your own organization's rules, and this app has no visibility into it.

It also does not cover copies of Receipts Express hosted by anyone else. The project is open source, so others may run modified versions; a fork could behave completely differently from what is described here.

2.What is stored on your device

All of it stays in your browser's own storage for this site. Nothing here is transmitted anywhere.

The app creates three IndexedDB databases and five local storage values, and every one of them is described below. Only the first database holds anything of yours; the other two hold the app's own cache bookkeeping and the text recognition model.

Browser database (IndexedDB)

A database named best-receipts holds three collections:

CollectionWhat it contains
reports Report name, creation date, optional trip start/end dates, daily meal allowance, project number, and any currency exchange rates you entered by hand.
expenses Title, merchant, amount, currency, date, category, your notes, personal-portion amount, and ordering position.
images The receipt photo itself, stored as a JPEG. Images are resized to a maximum of 1800 px and re-encoded before saving; for receipts you photograph or upload, that re-encoding also drops any EXIF metadata the original file carried, including a location tag.

Browser local storage

Five small values, and nothing else:

  • br.profile — the optional details you choose to print on exported PDFs: your name, employee ID, cost center, and project numbers. You type these yourself; they are never verified against anything or sent anywhere.
  • br.lastBackupAt — when you last exported a backup, so the app can remind you.
  • br.backupWarningDismissedAt and br.storageWarningDismissedAt — when you last dismissed a reminder, so it doesn't reappear immediately.
  • br.termsAccepted — the date you acknowledged the Terms of Use and this policy, and which version you saw, so the app doesn't ask again until something material changes.

Offline cache

The app's service worker keeps a copy of the app's own files so it can run without a connection. The code and icons are stored when you install the app. The text recognition and PDF engines are too large to store at install, so each is stored the first time you use it; these policy pages are stored the first time you open them. No receipt data is stored there.

Cache bookkeeping database

The caching library that manages that offline copy keeps a second, small IndexedDB database of its own, named workbox-expiration, with one collection, cache-entries. It records which of the app's own files were cached and when, so stale copies can be replaced on schedule. It holds file addresses and timestamps only — no receipt data, no expense data, and nothing about you. It is listed here because you will see it if you inspect your browser's storage, and an undocumented database is exactly the kind of thing this page should not leave unexplained.

Text recognition language database

The first time you scan a receipt, the text recognition engine fetches its English language data from this app's own address, unpacks it, and stores it in a third IndexedDB database so that later scans do not have to fetch it again. The stored copy is large — roughly 22 MB — and sits under a single entry keyed ./eng.traineddata. The engine caches through the idb-keyval library and takes that library's default names rather than anything this app chose, so the database is called keyval-store and its one collection keyval. It holds the recognition model and nothing else: no receipt data, no transcribed text, and nothing about you. It does not exist until your first scan, which is why a fresh install shows only the other two.

3.What is never collected

  • No account. There is no sign-up, sign-in, password, or email address. The app never asks who you are.
  • No analytics or telemetry. No usage statistics, no event tracking, no crash or error reporting. Errors are shown to you on-screen and go no further.
  • No advertising and no advertising SDKs.
  • No cookies. The app never sets or reads one.
  • No tracking identifiers. No device ID, advertising ID, session ID, or fingerprinting. Records get random IDs purely so the local database can reference them.
  • No location data. The app never requests geolocation.
  • No payments. There is no purchase, subscription, or billing of any kind.
  • No full receipt transcript. Text recognition reads the whole receipt in order to find the merchant, date, and total, but only those three values are passed to the form for you to review. The full transcribed text is discarded as soon as recognition finishes and is never saved.

4.Selling, sharing, and tracking

The author does not sell, share, rent, trade, or otherwise disclose your personal information — for any purpose, including cross-context behavioral advertising — and never has. There is no personal information in the author's possession to sell or share. This is a consequence of how the app is built, not merely a promise: there is no server, and your data never leaves your device.

Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. The app does not track you across websites or over time, so there is no tracking behavior for such a signal to turn off. No third party conducts tracking through this app.

Because your receipts can reveal health information — a pharmacy purchase, for instance — Washington's My Health My Data Act deserves a direct answer rather than silence. It gets its own page: the Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy.

5.Text recognition and PDF reading happen on your device

Receipts are read by Tesseract.js, and PDF receipts are converted to images by PDF.js. Both engines run inside your browser, and both are served from this app's own address — the app does not load code from external CDNs, and your receipts are never uploaded to a cloud recognition service.

This is the single most common place other receipt apps send your data. Here, the image never leaves the page it was opened in.

6.The one network interaction: loading the app

The app is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you open or update it, your browser fetches the app's files from GitHub, and GitHub — like any web host — necessarily sees the ordinary details of that request: your IP address, your browser's user agent, and the time. That handling is governed by GitHub's Privacy Statement, not by this policy, and it is outside this project's control.

This is the only third party involved, and it only ever sees a request for the app itself — never your receipts. Most of the app works offline once it is installed: the screens, your stored reports, and exporting them. The text recognition and PDF engines are the exception. They are too large to store at install, so your first scan and your first PDF receipt each need a connection to fetch the engine — again from GitHub Pages, and again a request for the app's own files, not for your data. After each has run once, it works offline too.

Your browser helps hold that boundary rather than leaving it to a promise. The deployed app carries a Content-Security-Policy — connect-src 'self' is the directive that matters here — which restricts fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket, EventSource and beacon requests to the app's own origin, and blocks form submissions to other origins. That closes the routes a script would ordinarily use to send data off your device, including a script smuggled in through a compromised dependency.

What it does not do. The policy is delivered in a <meta> tag, because GitHub Pages cannot send custom response headers, so it does not govern top-level navigation — a compromised dependency could still navigate the page elsewhere. A <meta> policy also reaches only the document that carries it, so each document needs its own. The app's document has one, and the standalone pages under docs/, including this one, now carry one too: the build derives a policy per page from the same source as the app's and injects it as the page is copied, relaxed only as far as that page's own inline stylesheet and any inline script it contains require. The restriction on where a page may send network requests is not relaxed on any of them. It is a strong control, not an absolute one. An automated test asserts that the app and each of these pages ships with a policy on every build.

7.Device permissions

  • Camera and photos. Adding a receipt opens your device's standard camera or file picker, one receipt at a time, at your request. The app has no continuous camera access and never opens a live camera stream — your browser's own permission prompt governs this.
  • Files. Choosing a photo, a PDF receipt, or a backup file to restore uses the same standard picker. The app can only read the file you pick.
  • Sharing. When you export, the app may hand the finished file to your device's share sheet so you can choose where it goes. You choose the destination, not the app.
  • Persistent storage. The app asks your browser to mark its storage as persistent, which makes automatic cleanup less likely to delete your reports. This is a request about durability; it transmits nothing.

The app never requests your microphone, location, contacts, notifications, or clipboard.

No biometrics. The camera photographs documents, nothing else. The app does not perform face recognition, fingerprint recognition, or any other biological measurement, and it does not collect, generate, enroll, or store any biometric identifier as defined in RCW 19.375.010.

8.Exporting and sharing

PDF reports, CSV summaries, and full JSON backups are all generated inside your browser and handed straight to your device's download or share mechanism. There is no upload step at any point.

Once you save or send an exported file, it is an ordinary file on your device or in whatever system you sent it to. Exported receipts still contain personal and financial information, so handle them according to your own organization's data-handling rules. This app has no visibility into, or control over, what happens to a file after it leaves the app.

9.Your control over your data

You hold the only copy, so you control it directly:

  • Take it with you. The Backup card on the home screen exports everything — every report, expense, and receipt image — as a single JSON file you can restore later or keep elsewhere. Individual reports also export as PDF or CSV.
  • Delete a report. Deleting a report also deletes all of its expenses and their receipt images.
  • Delete an expense. Deleting an expense also deletes its receipt images. Replacing a receipt image deletes the one it replaced.
  • Erase everything. There is currently no single "delete all data" button in the app. To remove everything at once, use your browser's own "clear site data" or website-data controls for this site. That erases all three databases, the stored settings, and the offline cache together.

Deletion is immediate and local. There is no server copy, no backup held by the author, and no retention period — once it is gone from your device, it is gone.

10.How your data is protected — and what that does not mean

Your data sits in your browser's storage sandbox, which keeps it separate from other websites and other apps. It is not separately encrypted by this app. In practice, what protects it is your device: your screen lock, your account password, and your device's disk encryption. Treat the app the way you would treat a folder of paper receipts in your bag.

That sandbox is scoped to a web address, not to an app: anything else served from the same address shares this storage and can read it. GitHub Pages puts every project of an account at one address, each under its own path, so another page published there is a neighbour of this app's data rather than a stranger to it. The path does not separate them — only a different address would.

Anyone with access to your unlocked device and browser profile can open the app and read what is in it. There is no separate app passcode. Because receipts can carry merchant names, dates, amounts, and partial payment card numbers, use the app only on a device you control and trust, with a device passcode and disk encryption enabled — not on a shared or public computer.

Breach notification. Washington's data breach notification law, chapter 19.255 RCW, places its duties on whoever owns or licenses data containing personal information, with a narrower duty on whoever maintains data owned by someone else. The author holds no such data — there is no server, no log, and no backup — so there is no store of your data that could be breached. What the author can commit to instead: if he ever learns that the hosted app has been modified to transmit your data, a prominent notice will go on this page and in the project's public repository as promptly as reasonably possible.

Your data can also disappear on its own

Browser storage is not guaranteed to last. Clearing site data, switching browsers or devices, or an operating-system storage cleanup can erase it permanently — on iOS, Safari may clear it after roughly seven days without a visit. There is no server copy to restore from. Receipts Express is a capture-and-export tool, not an archive: export your report as soon as it is complete, and back up regularly.

11.Your privacy rights

Privacy rights such as access, correction, portability, and erasure normally apply to an organization that holds data about you. This project holds none: it never receives your receipts, never stores them, and keeps no record that you use the app at all.

So there is nothing for the author to look up, hand over, correct, or delete on your behalf. The equivalent capabilities are built into the app itself and described in section 9 — you exercise them directly, without asking anyone.

That answer is the same wherever you live. State consumer privacy laws place their duties on businesses and controllers that collect personal information; around twenty states now have one, and they are not uniform — some key applicability to revenue or volume thresholds, others to small-business status. Which test a given state uses does not change the answer here, because the answer does not rest on a threshold: the author collects none and receives none, and so is neither a business nor a controller under any of them.

If you are in the EEA or the United Kingdom. The General Data Protection Regulation and the UK GDPR place their duties on a controller — the party that decides why and how personal data is processed — and on a processor acting on a controller's instructions. The author is neither. Your receipts, the text read from them, and everything you type never leave your device and never reach him, so there is no personal data of yours for him to control or process. The one exception is the ordinary web-server record described in section 6: GitHub, which hosts the app, sees your IP address, your browser, and the time when your browser downloads it. That handling is governed by GitHub's Privacy Statement rather than by this policy, and the author cannot see, configure, or retrieve any of it. This is a description of what happens, not a claim of certification — no regulator has reviewed this app, and if you think this description is wrong, the contact route below is the fastest way to say so.

Washington's My Health My Data Act gets a fuller, separate answer on the Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy page.

If you think anything on this page is inaccurate, please say so — open an issue using the contact link below. An error here matters more than a bug in the app.
